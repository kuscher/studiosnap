What we're building

One app that owns capture, with a floating bar as its face

The whole product hangs off a floating toolbar. It appears over whatever is on screen when you press the Screenshot key, and everything else — selection, result cards, pins, the editor — flows from it. The feasibility study proved on your HP Googlebook that this works: capture in 7–16 ms with no prompt, the bar can take over the screenshot keys, and its overlays sit above the taskbar.

What it does

  • Area, window, whole screen, scrolling and text capture
  • Auto-copy and auto-save, a floating result card, pins on top
  • A real editor: arrows, steps, blur, redaction, beautify frames
  • Screen recording to MP4, GIF and WebP
  • A searchable library of everything you've captured

How it earns trust

  • Open source, MIT, no internet permission in the capture app
  • The accessibility service does the work; a plain screen explains it before Android's dialog
  • Key takeover is a switch you can leave off
  • Redaction is solid by default, because blur can be reversed
  • Runs in a picker-only mode if you decline accessibility

Where the design comes from

CleanShot X and Snagit for the flow and the editor, ShareX for power, Material 3 Expressive for the native Googlebook look. The bar follows the Expressive floating-toolbar spec; Compose doesn't ship that component yet, so we build our own to match.

Surface 1 · the toolbar

The capture bar

A 64 dp floating pill, centered 16 dp above the taskbar. Only the chosen mode and source carry a text label; everything else is an icon with a tooltip that names its shortcut key. It moves to the top edge when your selection needs the space, and you can drag it anywhere by the grip. Below are the states it takes, on light and dark content, rendered at real size.

Preview theme

The parts, left to right:

  1. Grip. Drag to move the bar; it snaps to the top or bottom edge. Double-click resets.
  2. Mode. Screenshot or Record. R switches.
  3. Source. Area, Window, Screen, Scrolling, Text. Record mode shows only the first three.
  4. Timer and options. Off, 3, 5 or 10 s. Options hold freeze, pointer, shadow and what happens after capture.
  5. Last area. Re-captures the previous region. Also Ctrl + Screenshot key.
  6. Capture. The one filled button. It turns red for Record and shows the countdown when a timer runs.
  7. Collapse and close. Collapsed, the bar keeps only mode and Capture. Esc closes.

Text

Copy text from anything

Text mode reads the exact text that apps expose through accessibility, so there are no OCR typos. Pixels without text data go through on-device OCR. QR codes and links become buttons. Copied automatically.

Scrolling

Long captures, stitched

Pick a window; StudioSnap scrolls it about ⅔ of a page at a time, grabs the window and stitches as it goes. A live preview strip grows beside it. Sticky headers are dropped. Manual mode lets you scroll yourself for apps that ignore scroll commands.

Recording

A small pill while recording

After Android's share picker and a 3-2-1 countdown, the bar shrinks to this pill: time, pause, stop, mic, camera bubble, draw and discard. A dashed outline sits just outside the recorded area so it never lands in the video. Clicks and shortcuts can show as ripples and keycaps.

Quick markup

Annotate in place

With "Mark up before saving" on, a compact tool strip docks to the edge of your selection: arrow, box, highlight, pen, text, step number, redact, undo, done. The markup stays editable later in Studio.

Ways in

Keys, taskbar, tile, launcher

The Screenshot key or Action Shift S. A second launcher icon, StudioSnap Capture, opens the bar in one click. There's a Quick Settings tile and long-press shortcuts: Area, Window, Screen, Record, Text.

Surface 10

Studio, the editor

A normal resizable window, opened from a result card, a pin or the library. The tools sit in a floating strip at the top — the same pill as the capture bar — with a property bar beneath for the active tool. Frame and Layers live in the right panel. Every annotation stays editable after saving.

Frame

Gradient or wallpaper backgrounds, padding, rounded corners and shadow, plus sizes for slides and social posts. "Auto" picks a background from the screenshot's own edge colours.

Safe by default

Redaction exports as a solid fill, because blur and pixelation can be reversed. Export flattens the image and strips metadata. Auto-redact suggests emails, phone numbers, card numbers and API keys for you to confirm.

Keyboard

Default keys

"Action" is the Googlebook's G key (Meta in Android). With key takeover on, StudioSnap sees these first and the system does nothing. With takeover off, bind Action + a key to "StudioSnap Capture" in the Shortcut Helper (Action / → Customize), and StudioSnap never sees your typing.

How it's built

One APK: a service that owns capture, and windows that open on demand

The accessibility service is always ready. It routes keys, grabs frames and hosts every overlay in Compose. Studio, Library, Settings and first run are ordinary activities. Recording runs in its own foreground service.

How I test

  • On your HP Googlebook over adb, with a ./ss helper like the other Book projects
  • Hotkeys through a virtual keyboard (uinput), the same path as real keys; never types into your windows
  • Visual checks capture only StudioSnap's own overlays, never your apps
  • Unit tests for the annotation model, stitcher, GIF encoder and naming; CI on GitHub Actions with the x86_64 emulator, never on the VM

Speed targets

  • Key press to visible bar: under 100 ms
  • Release to image on clipboard: under 350 ms at full screen
  • Window capture: under 50 ms
  • Studio open: under 500 ms
  • Scrolling: about 3 frames a second, the Android limit

Risks and open checks

What I'll check first

Check

System clipboard preview. SystemUI has its own clipboard overlay. If it shows when StudioSnap copies, our card has to move clear of it.

Check

Drag out of an overlay window. Android's source allows it; first thing tested in Phase 1.

Fallback: drag from the Library window.

Check

Mouse hover and right-click on overlays, and the system font by name (google-sans-flex).

Fallbacks exist for both: click-to-grab, and Roboto Flex.

Medium

Key takeover sees all typing. It has to be fast and trustworthy.

Handler returns immediately, nothing logged, no network permission, open source, and a no-takeover mode through the Shortcut Helper.

Medium

Sideload setup. A Chrome-downloaded APK needs "Allow restricted settings" before accessibility.

An illustrated guide in first run and the README; adb installs skip it on this device.

Low

Intel Googlebooks. Same APIs, untested on x86_64 hardware.

x86_64 emulator tests in CI from Phase 0.
