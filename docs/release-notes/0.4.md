Screen recordings now have sound, and a camera bubble of you. Everything still runs on your Googlebook: StudioSnap has no INTERNET permission.

**Download:** grab `StudioSnap.apk` below. It installs over 0.3 and keeps your settings. See the README for the one-time "Allow restricted settings" step if this is your first install.

## New in 0.4

- **Voice-overs and app sound.** In Record mode, turn on the **microphone** for a voice-over and/or **system audio** for what your apps play. Both are mixed into the recording, in sync with the video.
- **Camera bubble.** Turn on the **camera** in Record mode for a live, mirrored bubble of you, burned into the recording like on ChromeOS. It shows up before you press Record, so you can frame yourself.
  - Drag it anywhere; drop it near a corner and it snaps in.
  - Hover or tap it for size, circle or rounded square, and switching cameras when a webcam is plugged in.
  - **Remove background** leaves just you, head and body, floating over the screen.
- **Click while you record.** The recording controls no longer block the rest of the screen.
- **Stop from the notification.** The recording notification has a Stop button.
- The bar's settings button now opens Settings.

## Permissions

- **New:** microphone and camera. Android asks only when you first turn on the mic, system audio or camera toggle. System audio never uses the mic.
- **Removed:** INTERNET. Earlier versions (0.1 to 0.3) carried it by accident: a library bundled with the text recognition added it, even though StudioSnap never used the network. It's gone now, so the "nothing leaves your Googlebook" promise holds for the app itself too.

## Fixes

- Granting a permission no longer closes the capture bar; it steps aside for the dialog and comes back as it was.
- Closing StudioSnap while the screen-sharing prompt is up no longer leaves it stuck "about to record".
- Recordings end exactly at Stop, save failures are reported instead of showing "Saved", and a full disk stops the recording cleanly.

## Known limits

- With the mic and system audio both on and no headphones, your speakers echo into the mic.
- Background removal is good but not perfect around hair.
- The see-through area around a cut-out bubble still takes clicks (Android can't shape an overlay's touch area).
